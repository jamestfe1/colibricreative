The work

02 / 06

01 · Boutique law firm serving underserved clients · Jul 2026–present

Helping the people who need a lawyer find this one.

A firm with the expertise and the mission, but no front door. As its sole operations partner, I'm taking its digital presence from concept to launch, and building it so the firm can run it without me.

Brand identity
Logo, color palette, typography, and voice guidelines that make a small firm look as trustworthy as it is.
Website, concept to launch
Sitemap, practice-area pages, and plain-language content written for clients who may be meeting a lawyer for the first time.
Bilingual intake bridge
Clients can reach the firm in their own language — English, Spanish, and Portuguese. It routes and informs, with guardrails so it never gives individualized legal advice. Built on the Gemini API.
Accessibility
Built to WCAG 2.1 AA: contrast, keyboard navigation, and screen-reader labels, because the people least served online are often the ones a firm like this exists for.
Local findability
Google Business Profile, domain, and professional email set up so search leads to the firm, not a directory.
Knowledge infrastructure
Standardized templates and contract resources, so institutional knowledge is findable by whoever needs it next.
AI, only as far as enough
Each tool is weighed against a real manual, repeatable task, and each one runs under attorney oversight.
One shared view
Every workstream tracked in ClickUp, so the attorneys can see status without asking me.

The practice

03 / 06

Three habits, one way of working. I start with where the time goes, build so the work doesn't depend on me, and bring in technology only as far as it's needed.

01.

See where the time goes

I trace each process back to the people and hours it consumes, so a team can see the system it's actually running.

02.

Build it to run without me

I connect people, signals, and tools so the system is trusted by the team, and so it still works the day I leave.

03.

Technology, only as far as enough

AI and automation where they return real hours, never where they add risk or hide decisions from the people accountable for them.

Why a hummingbird

04 / 06

Colibri is the word for hummingbird in Portuguese, my first language. Hummingbirds live only in the Americas, and so does my story: from Brazil to New Jersey, working across Portuguese, English, and Spanish.

They're small, and the systems around them depend on them. Moving from flower to flower, a hummingbird carries what one part of the system needs to the next. That's the work I love: connecting legal, creative, operations, and clients so nothing gets lost in between.

And they know exactly how much energy they have. A hummingbird spends it with precision and rests to save it. I build the same way: every hour a system takes should give more hours back, and no team should need more technology than is enough.
